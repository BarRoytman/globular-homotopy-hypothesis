There are three ``relative" variants of this cylinder construction that we will need.

The $n$-simplex $\Delta^n$ for $n \in \Nat$ is inductively defined by $\Delta^0 = G^0$, $\Delta^1 = G^1$ with $\Delta^0$ regarded as the $\sigma^0_1$ subcomplex of $\Delta^1$ and $\Delta^{n+2} = \Cyl(\Delta^{n+1}, \Delta^{n})$ for all $n \in \Nat$ with $\Delta^{n+1}$ regarded as the bottom subcomplex of $\Delta^{n+2}$. 

The centric subdivision $M_n$ for $n \in \Nat$ is the CW-complex having exactly one $k$-cell $e_{(\alpha_i)_{i \in [k]}}$ for each strictly ascending chain $(\alpha_i)_{i \in [k]}$ of cells of $G^n$ (ascending in the sense of their subcomplexes ascending) with the standard cell structure (and no other cells). Moreover, there for each $k$-cell $e_{(\alpha_i)_{i \in [k]}}$ there exists a subcomplex $X_{(\alpha_i)_{i \in [k]}}$ consisting of precisely the cells $e_{(\alpha_{\nu(j)})_{j \in [h]}}$ for every strictly monotone map $\nu \colon [h] \to [k]$ for $h \leq k$. The structure of the subcomplex is inductively determined by $X_{(\alpha_i)_{i \in [k + 1]}} ~\cong \Cyl(X_{(\alpha_i)_{i \in [k]}}, X_{(\alpha_i)_{i \in [k - 1]}})$ such that $X_{(\alpha_i)_{i \in [k]}} \to \Cyl(X_{(\alpha_i)_{i \in [k]}}, X_{(\alpha_i)_{i \in [k - 1]}})$ \\$\cong X_{(\alpha_i)_{i \in [k + 1]}}$ is the standard inclusion and the passing to subsequences to contain $k+1$ yields compatible equivalences with $e_{(\alpha_i)_{i \in [k]}}$ as the highest dimensional cell.

(There is a nicer way to write the above construction.)

The $M_n$ form a coglobular globular set.

The total element of $M_n$ is inductively defined as follows. For $M_0$ the total element $t_0$ is the universial element. Set $u_0 = (\rho_0)^* (e_{\tau^0_1, \id_1}$ and $v_0 = e_{(\sigma^0_1, \id_1)}$.
(For $M_1$ the total element is $t_1 = (c^0_{1,1})^* \langle v_0, u_0 \rangle$.) In general, the construction involves functorialiaty of the relative cylinder.
